01 SAE J300 · Viscosity decoding

What 10W-40, 15W-40 and 20W-50 are really saying.

The first number with W tells you about cold-start flow and how easily the oil moves at low temperature. The second number reflects viscosity at operating temperature, which affects film strength, wear protection and how the lubricant behaves under load.

0W-20 / 5W-30 fast cold-start flow and fuel efficiency.
10W-40 balanced grade with broader temperature protection.
15W-40 popular heavy-duty grade for load and durability.
20W-50 thicker film strength for severe service or hotter environments.
Also included in the graphic: ISO VG 32 / 46 / 68 / 150 / 220 / 320, additive-package basics and used-oil analysis signals such as viscosity shift, water, oxidation and TAN/TBN.
02 Industrial lubrication

Gears, bearings and film strength for industrial reliability.

Industrial lubrication decisions depend on speed, load, temperature, contamination and equipment design. In practice, the job is to select the right base oil and viscosity so that the lubricant forms a stable film, reduces wear and supports uptime.

Gearboxes higher load conditions often move toward ISO VG 150 / 220 / 320.
Hydraulics ISO VG 32 / 46 / 68 are common, depending on duty and climate.
Bearings need the right film thickness plus contamination control.
Tribology lens viscosity is selected for machine condition, not in isolation.
03 Heavy-duty diesel oils

HD oils have to manage soot, load, detergency and drain confidence.

In heavy-duty applications the lubricant is not only reducing friction. It is also controlling deposits, neutralising acids, holding contaminants in suspension and protecting components under high temperature and load.

Common workhorse grade 15W-40 remains a familiar reference point in many fleets.
Additive depth detergent, dispersant, anti-wear and antioxidant chemistry matter.
Application fit duty cycle, ambient temperature and OEM guidance influence grade choice.
Commercial credibility explaining why a grade fits a duty profile builds trust.
04 Marine lubrication

Marine engines and shipboard systems demand a different lubrication conversation.

Marine lubrication goes beyond one universal oil. Different systems and engine types have different needs: trunk piston engines, crosshead engines, cylinder oils, system oils, gears, hydraulics and compressors each involve their own operating logic.

Trunk piston engines crankcase oil must handle detergency, wear and oxidation control.
Crosshead engines cylinder lubrication and system lubrication are separate conversations.
Ship systems hydraulics, gears and compressors still need correct viscosity and cleanliness.
Marine credibility technical clarity helps commercial discussions land better with customers.
